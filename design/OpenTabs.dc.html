Open tabs

A little of everything that makes me, me.

Not everything I’m curious about fits neatly into a job title. Some things I explore because I want to understand them, some because they catch my attention, and others simply because I enjoy them.

This is where I keep those little rabbit holes, interesting finds, things I’m learning, and ideas I can’t quite leave alone. Think of it as a peek at the tabs I keep open, both in my browser and in my mind.

  • Content creation

I create for the person I used to be.

When I was finding my way into tech, I had plenty of questions and often had to piece together the answers myself. That experience shaped the kind of content I want to create today: the kind that helps someone feel a little less lost at the beginning.

Whether I’m sharing something I’ve learned, documenting my journey, or breaking down an unfamiliar idea, I want people to leave with something useful and a clearer sense of what they can do next.

Next tab: Writing
  • Writing

Some thoughts need somewhere to land.

I like writing because it gives me room to sit with an idea, ask better questions, and work out what I actually think. Sometimes that means explaining something I’ve been learning; other times, it means reflecting on an experience or noticing a connection I hadn’t seen before.

That’s part of why I started Ripples & Reverie, my newsletter about content, community, and clarity in motion. It’s a place to look at what’s happening around me and make sense of what it might mean.

Ripples explores ideas and developments moving through tech, content, and community. Reverie turns inward, making room for reflection and the lessons that emerge along the way.

Next tab: Open source
  • Open source

I believe more people should get to be part of building things.

My interest in open source began when I was a software developer. I was drawn to the idea that people could learn from one another, contribute to shared projects, and build alongside people they might never meet in person.

Over time, that interest grew beyond writing code. Through community work, volunteering, and helping beginners find their footing, I became just as interested in what makes open source welcoming and accessible as I was in the technology itself.

I still care about helping people see that they don’t need to know everything before they begin. Sometimes, the first step is finding a project, asking a question, or making that first contribution.

Next tab: Volunteering
  • Volunteering

I started by giving back. I didn’t expect how much I’d learn along the way.

Volunteering has given me opportunities to show up in ways that don’t always come with a title or a spotlight. Sometimes that has meant helping an event run smoothly; other times, it has meant teaching a beginner, documenting a moment, or making sure someone feels supported.

Each experience has reminded me that meaningful communities are built through the work people are willing to do for one another, including the small things that happen behind the scenes.
